OUT-OF-MODEL:
  - Client-side Unicode handling after delivery can synthesize the adjacency
    the chokepoint checks for. The documented property is deliberately two
    adjacent ASCII characters ("The property is about two characters rather
    than about markdown", security.md:604-605), and breakLinkAdjacency is a
    literal `text.replace("](", "] (")`. A chat client that NFKC-normalizes
    (fullwidth `］（` → `](`) or strips zero-width / bidi-control characters
    before linkifying (`]​(`, bidi-wrapped pairs) would form the link after
    the Provider's raw-byte check has run. Notable because the sanitizer's
    own canonical-form pass exists precisely on the admission that
    canonicalization synthesizes link syntax (security.md:552-564) — that
    reasoning stops at the chokepoint. The threat model's renderer model
    ("a renderer resolves a link only on the adjacent pair") places this
    outside the documented commitments; flagging in case the model should
    say something about post-delivery normalization.
  - Reference-style and collapsed markdown links (`[label][ref]` resolved
    by a `[ref]: https://...` definition) hide a URL behind a label through
    a `][` adjacency the property does not cover. Multi-post assembly (the
    degraded digest joins several mutually untrusted publishers into one
    body, security.md:516-536) lets one author supply the label and another
    the definition, synthesizing a label-hiding link across operands even
    with the chokepoint working exactly as designed. Outside the documented
    `](` commitment; whether v1 clients render reference links decides if
    the model needs extending.
