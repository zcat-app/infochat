RED-TEAM VERDICT: CLEAN

OUT-OF-MODEL:
  - SimpleXMentionParser's three-tier decode fallback (URL-safe base64 → standard base64 → UTF-8 literal) could in principle produce a cross-encoding collision where two different strings decode to the same bytes, allowing a forged mention. Not reachable in practice because real SimpleX queue addresses are cryptographic (attacker cannot choose their own decoded bytes), the SimpleXMessageCodec validator rejects `+` and `/` from inbound memberRefs (eliminating the URL-safe-vs-standard divergence path), and the bot's queue address is operator-set trusted config. Surface narrows to a theoretical concern only.
  - Log-injection via attacker-controlled `chatType` field landing in DEBUG `Ignored` reason strings at SimpleXMessageCodec.java line 296 and SimpleXWebSocketClient.java line 295. Only reachable when production lowers the SimpleX logger below the baseline; out-of-model per security.md "production logger-level baseline MUST keep these categories at WARN or above."
  - Unbounded mention-list growth in SimpleXMessageCodec.extractMentionQueueAddresses — no per-frame cap on the number of mention entries processed. Bounded in practice by the 1 MiB WebSocket frame cap (MAX_FRAME_BYTES) and by transport-level rate caps the Provider enforces at authorization step 1.5; not a new gap introduced by this diff.
